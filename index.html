⚡ TOPIC 1: RISK-TAKING & IMPULSIVE DECISIONS
Why do teenagers make quick, risky decisions without thinking?

[FACT 1] The prefrontal cortex,the area responsible for planning, risk analysis, and logic—is not fully developed until around age 25!
[FACT 2] Teenagers rely more on the amygdala (the emotional center), making them act on immediate emotional impulses rather than long-term logic.

😴 TOPIC 2: SLEEP & THE BIOLOGICAL CLOCK
Teenagers are not lazy—their internal circadian rhythms actually shift during puberty!

[FACT 3] The brain hormone melatonin (which induces sleepiness) is released about 2 hours later at night in teens than in adults or children.
[FACT 4] Teen brains require 8 to 10 hours of sleep each night to clean out cellular waste and consolidate memories from the day.

📚 TOPIC 3: LEARNING & MEMORY REWIRING
During adolescence, the brain undergoes a structural overhaul and builds new neural pathways.

[FACT 5] The teen brain undergoes synaptic pruning—it deletes weak or unused connections while strengthening active ones.
[FACT 6] Because of high neuroplasticity, teenagers can learn new complex skills (languages, instruments, sports) faster than adults.

🍎 TOPIC 4: FOOD & BRAIN ENERGY
The brain requires clean nutrition and energy to construct new neural pathways.

[FACT 7] The brain makes up only about 2% of body weight, but consumes over 20% of the body's total glucose energy!
[FACT 8] Essential fatty acids like Omega-3 build myelin (the insulating sheath on brain wiring), keeping signal transmissions running fast.

💡 5 USEFUL ADVICE TIPS FOR TEENAGERS 💡
1. Respect the Sleep schedule: Go to sleep at steady hours instead of going to sleep at random hours

2. Pause for 5 Seconds: When faced with a sudden risky choice, count to 5 to let your slower prefrontal cortex evaluate what your fast amygdala wants to do.

3. Practice What You Want to Keep: Because of synaptic pruning, skills you practice during these years get hard-wired into your brain for life.

4. Fuel with Good Fats: Eat nuts, seeds, and healthy oils to provide the Omega-3 building blocks needed for fast neural pathways.

5. Drink Plenty of Water: Dehydration directly reduces attention span and short-term memory performance during school hours.
